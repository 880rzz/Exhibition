01 / Exhibition story

Twenty photographs. Personal perspectives. One shared encounter.

Community photography exhibition · 2025

The Image of My Life

A photographer creates thousands of pictures over a lifetime. What happens when they are asked to choose the one image that carries the greatest personal meaning? The Image of My Life explored that question.

Presented by the Vienna Hungarian School Photography Club, the exhibition brought together twenty photographs attributed to fifteen Hungarian photographers at CITYgalleryVIENNA. Different life paths and points of view converged around personal images.

The exhibition sought to open a conversation between artist and viewer, connecting Hungarian photographers living in different countries.

Loksi67

Artwork

Wind over the Flooded Lake

Editorial title based on the artwork description

Title recorded in the source: Wild is the Wind

Prices in the submitted 2025 text are archival, not current offers.

Editorial translation of the submitted 2025 text

Price listed in 2025: €250. Not a current offer.

1. Work description (context)

Wild is the Wind was made at a particular moment and place. Around Christmas 2024, the Danube was high and the groundwater in the Csallóköz region had risen unusually far. At the flooded gravel-pit lake at Lakszakállas, wind and water gave the landscape a surreal atmosphere. The photographer set out not to document the event but to interpret it and catch the unrestrained energy of the elements.

The original gives the work’s location and year as Csallóköz, 2024. The title conveys the image’s energy and the photographer’s choice to give up some control deliberately.

2. Technique and philosophy

The photographer used redscale film, loaded back to front. Its base acts as a colour filter, giving the view deep, hot reds and oranges. The photograph was taken with a homemade pinhole camera, or camera obscura, with a curved back that widens the perspective. The exposure of about 40 seconds records the action of water, light and wind slowly, softening the boundary of an instant.

The pinhole camera and redscale film are a protest against digital perfection. Working by hand and entirely in analogue, the photographer lets light and chemistry, chance and intuition shape the image together. The surreal result comes from the interaction between intention and technique.

3. Composition and mood

With the pinhole camera’s curved back, the space stretches at the edges and seems to wrap around the viewer, making the landscape feel organic and dreamlike.

The long exposure softens the water and movement in the sky into painterly blur. The landscape becomes a single glowing vision. The redscale colours act as an emotional filter, conveying a melancholy that is both unsettling and peaceful, and the anxiety and humility that nature’s power can evoke. The picture becomes a metaphor for the beauty and transience of the world.

A Memory of Val d’Orcia

Editorial title based on the artwork description

Title recorded in the source: Toscana Dream (Val d’Orcia, 2016)

Prices in the submitted 2025 text are archival, not current offers.

Editorial translation of the submitted 2025 text

Price listed in 2025: €250. Not a current offer.

1. Work description: Val d’Orcia – Nostalgia Encoded

2. Title: Toscana Dream (Val d’Orcia, 2016)

3. Technique and philosophy: This photograph is the memory of a journey both personal and experimental. I took it in Tuscany in 2016, in the beautiful Orcia valley, on my first holiday with my wife.

I deliberately chose expired Ektachrome 200 slide film. Cross-processing gave the image its distinctive character: the film, designed for the E-6 process, was developed in C-41 chemistry.

The cross-processing produced strong, distinct grain and an unusual colour shift. The rows of Tuscan cypresses took on unexpected, dreamlike tones, heightening the landscape’s melancholy, surreal atmosphere.

4. Composition and mood: A classic view of the Orcia valley creates a dramatic contrast. An even, timeless line of trees leads the eye into the distance, while the painterly cloud formations filling the sky are essential to the effect. The photograph does not merely document the scene; it rewrites the memory. Cross-processing becomes a visual encoding of nostalgia, adding a richer emotional layer to reality.

About the artist

Editorial translation of the submitted 2025 text

Stopping time, the analogue way

I have been recording reality since 1983. I am one of those “analogue dinosaurs” who still work with analogue cameras and classic film processes, whether with negatives or slides.

Developing a black-and-white negative is a sacred ritual for me. There is no deeper creative experience than watching a work come into being by my own hands, as chemistry and light meet in the developing tank. This hands-on process is photography in its original, essential form. Every time I develop a film it is a different experience.

One particular advantage of analogue storage is that the images can be viewed without a digital device, are easy to keep over the long term and can be reproduced without difficulty. You can even enjoy a slide by candlelight.

The primacy of the moment

The work begins in the viewfinder. I compose the image before making the exposure, through the interplay of subjects, light and shadow. Only when I have fully formed the image in my mind do I release the shutter. The camera is a tool for looking deliberately, not for correcting things later.

I make my photographs without digital manipulation. A photograph should be a photograph, not an exercise in Photoshop acrobatics, moving sliders back and forth or cutting and pasting different backgrounds. In my view, that takes neither a photographer’s eye nor talent. I would call this sort of image-making digital montage rather than photography.

Mission

The world is a wonder in itself. My artistic mission is to preserve as many remarkable moments as I can for those who come after me, seen from my own point of view. I make photographs as a monument against the passing of time: breaths held still before a moment slips away like a drop of rain in a downpour.

Artwork

The Timeless Elegance of the Dirndl

Editorial title based on the artwork description

Title recorded in the source: Éljen a Dirndl

Prices in the submitted 2025 text are archival, not current offers.

Editorial translation of the submitted 2025 text

Price listed in 2025: €250. Not a current offer.

1. Work description (concept and context)

Éljen a Dirndl (Long Live the Dirndl) expresses respect for traditional dress, femininity and enduring elegance. HajNika’s photograph emphasises the symbolic power of the dirndl. More than a garment, it is a cultural symbol that can bring out the wearer’s confidence and quiet charm. For the photographer, the work celebrates the feeling that anyone wearing it can feel entirely at ease in her own femininity.

2. Title, technique and philosophy

The title is a declaration against passing fashions. As a cultural icon, the dirndl stands for something lasting in a world where trends change quickly.

HajNika values quality, honesty and character. The technique brings out the contrasts between materials such as silk, velvet and cloth. The light is soft but deliberate, with the emphasis on keeping an authentic look. Fine folds, the fitted bodice and the lightness of the apron (Schürze) form a visual conversation in which traditional dress frames a contemporary wearer.

3. Composition and mood

The sitter is placed centrally and gently separated from the surroundings. The symmetrical bodice (Mieder) and the carefully framed neckline draw the eye. The pose conveys dignity, poise and confidence.

The atmosphere is romantic, nostalgic and uplifting. The source suggests deep red and white tones, bringing a sense of gentle charm. The picture proposes that wearing a dirndl is not simply an echo of the past but an expression of self-possession and cultural identity. It celebrates the beauty and dignity the artist sees in femininity.

A Heart in the Candle Flame

Editorial title based on the artwork description

Title recorded in the source: Feuer und Flamme

Prices in the submitted 2025 text are archival, not current offers.

Editorial translation of the submitted 2025 text

Price listed in 2025: €250. Not a current offer.

1. Work description (context and vision)

In Feuer und Flamme, HajNika finds universal feelings in the intimate sight of a burning candle. The photograph records more than candlelight. It gives shape to an inner vision of love and peace in the dance of a flame. A glowing heart seems to merge with the candle, and a dove rises towards the light. Together they suggest hope, purity and feelings that endure. The image invites reflection, turning an outward light into a mirror of the inner world.

2. Title, technique and philosophy

The German title literally means fire and flame. As an expression it also conveys enthusiasm or passion for something.

Technique: Soft focus and careful lighting set the image apart. The bokeh, or softly blurred spots of light behind the candle, dissolves the surrounding space and directs attention to the central form and its light. The unobtrusive digital post-processing integrates the symbolic heart and dove without disrupting the atmosphere.

Philosophy: The candle carries a meaning beyond the object itself. Its flame suggests life, hope and transience; the heart and dove express love and peace. For HajNika, light can reveal emotional and spiritual layers beyond visible reality.

3. Composition and mood

The candle is placed at the centre, giving the symbol particular force. It is lit from below with a halo-like glow, as if shining from within. A heart carved into the candle and a dove that seems to rise from the flame make a still scene feel alive. Details that at first look subtle give the image several layers of meaning.

Warm golds suggest calm and the comfort of home. The dark, blurred background brings the candle’s radiance into focus and makes the scene meditative and peaceful. The viewer can almost feel the heat and pulse of the flame. The work asks us to slow down and look in the light for the things in ourselves that endure.

Léna Erdős

Artwork

The Colours of Hope

Editorial title based on the artwork description

Title recorded in the source: Remény – Hoffnung – Hope

Editorial translation of the submitted 2025 text

1. Work description (context)

Remény – Hoffnung – Hope brings together the spiritual and emotional concerns in Léna Erdős’s art. The image answers what the artist calls a summons from the soul. It is not a record of external reality, but a way of expressing an inner world, possibility and a fascination with colour. For Erdős, what comes from the heart and soul can become a work of art. This picture explores hope through form and atmosphere, carrying its message in three languages.

2. Title, technique and philosophy

The trilingual title expresses the artist’s conviction that the feeling can be shared across languages and cultures.

Technique: Colour and light play a central role. A long exposure or intentional camera movement (ICM) may have dissolved the scene into painterly patches. Soft edges and spreading light hold the feeling of a moment rather than its physical detail. The emphasis on colour, particularly the suggestion of warm, optimistic tones, reflects the artist’s interest in painting and blurs the line between the two media.

Philosophy: Erdős looks for possibility in everything. Blur and abstraction make room for potential, sensation and an inner reality rather than treating the image as a set of fixed facts. Her invitation to “See, feel, taste and create” applies here to hope as a fundamental feeling that crosses cultural and linguistic boundaries.

3. Composition and mood

Composition: The picture relies on dynamic abstraction. Where hard lines might have been, coloured light and tone form a single organic whole. Colour seems to gather towards the top, suggesting a rising current of light and a lifting of the spirit. That arrangement draws the viewer into a floating, emotional space.

Mood: The picture is warm, lively and freeing. The light and colours almost pulse, conveying calm and a sense of limitless optimism. The work presents unconditional hope as an answer to fear and uncertainty. This hope depends not on outside circumstances, but on the artist’s inward commitment and the joy of creating from the soul.

A Quiet Moment Before the Wedding

Editorial title based on the artwork description

Title recorded in the source: Ébredés

Editorial translation of the submitted 2025 text

1. Work description (context)

Ébredés – Erwachen – Awakening holds a brief pause amid the rush of wedding preparations. Before the ceremony, the photographer felt something new beginning, almost a miracle coming into being. The preparations, perhaps a mirror and make-up, suggest the occasion on the outside. The central figure’s posture and the way we see them evoke a private, reflective state. An outward event meets inward change.

2. Title, technique and philosophy

The trilingual title points to something people can share across languages: the feeling of standing on the threshold of a lifelong commitment.

Technique: The photograph may use soft natural window light and a shallow depth of field, perhaps from a wide aperture, to hold attention on the moment itself. A reflection, in a mirror or another glossy surface, could add to the composition and suggest the coexistence of an old and a new self. Softness or subtle grain lends the scene a dreamlike quality.

Philosophy: The image reflects on transition and renewal. Rather than concentrating on the ceremony as a public expression of love, it attends to an inward awakening. Pausing for a moment in the rush suggests that a turning point can become clear when we are present and quiet. It is a photograph about recognising what commitment and a sense of destiny can mean.

3. Composition and mood

Composition: The subject may be shown as a silhouette or a soft detail rather than in a direct portrait, suggesting an inward experience other people might recognise. A reflection, if present, would bring more than one point of view into the same scene. Soft textures and light and shade, perhaps in a dress or veil or a make-up light, create a romantic, uncluttered atmosphere.

Mood: The picture is intimate and full of anticipation. If rendered in colour, the tones suggest the gentleness of pastels or warm, soft light. The image feels like a breath in the quiet before a storm. Rush and stress give way to a sense of wonder and certainty. The stillness carries the promise of a new life about to begin.

Bödő Viktória

Artwork

A Fifth Encounter in Prague

Editorial title based on the artwork description

Prices in the submitted 2025 text are archival, not current offers.

Editorial translation of the submitted 2025 text

Price listed in 2025: €250. Not a current offer.

1. Work description (context)

This street portrait combines the photographer’s recurring attachment to Prague with a sense of nostalgia. To the photographer the unnamed street musician had become part of the city, like its cobblestones and cathedral spires. The image records their fifth encounter: a familiar situation, but a moment that cannot repeat. The sound of the trumpet takes visible form and becomes the sound of Prague itself, a marker of passing time and of continuity.

2. Title, technique and philosophy

A prágai utcazenész (The Prague Street Musician) is a simple title that hints at a personal connection built over repeated encounters.

Technique: A shallow depth of field appears to isolate the sharply rendered musician and instrument against soft bokeh. Street light, possibly at sunset or in the early evening, softens the tones and gives the image an intimate, timeless quality. The apparently warm palette reinforces the music’s melancholy and uplift.

Philosophy: The same place and person are there each time, but every moment is different. The image finds beauty in repetition and in catching what happens by chance. The photographer records not only the musician but also memories brought back to life by the trumpet. The result is a personal geography in which a person and a place cannot be separated.

3. Composition and mood

Composition: The musician appears to occupy a strong point in the composition. The hands around the trumpet and the face draw attention to the act of playing. Background details, perhaps part of a street or a building, suggest the setting without competing with the musician. A low or eye-level viewpoint would strengthen the intimacy.

Mood: The picture is dignified and intimate at once. The musician’s absorbed expression and the suggested sound of the trumpet convey quiet and nostalgia. The photograph preserves a thoughtful, personal encounter with Prague and makes the music seem to settle on the cobblestones as though it could last there forever.

Artwork

When the Smoke Rises

Editorial title based on the artwork description

Title recorded in the source: Füstös Finomság

Prices in the submitted 2025 text are archival, not current offers.

Editorial translation of the submitted 2025 text

Price listed in 2025: €250. Not a current offer.

1. Work description (context)

Füstös Finomság (Smoky Delicacy) catches a spontaneous yet refined moment from an impromptu evening out with friends. Instead of visiting the Christmas market, they went to a cocktail bar, where this little ritual took place. The picture holds the fraction of a second between anticipation and fulfilment: the dome over the cocktail is lifted slowly, and the smoke inside it dances around the drink. It was the best, priceless experience of the evening, born of the joy of being with friends and the impulse to take a photograph.

2. Title, technique and philosophy

The title points to the delicacy of the cocktail and the visual drama of the smoke.

Technique: The photograph depends on careful lighting and a fast shutter speed. Timing is crucial. The texture and translucency of the rising smoke depend on backlighting, whether from the understated lights in the bar or a light aimed specifically at it. A fast shutter speed preserves the moving spiral around the glass rather than letting the smoke blur into nothing. The drink is in sharp focus, while the surroundings dissolve into an intimate, private atmosphere.

Philosophy: The photograph treats a gastronomic experience as art. The cocktail is not merely a drink but a performance, a small marvel as rich to look at as it is to taste. Even the most unexpected moments can hold beauty and drama if the photographer is ready to catch them.

3. Composition and mood

Composition: The glass appears to sit near the centre of the composition or at a point set by the golden ratio. Wisps of smoke guide the eye towards the cocktail. The edges seem to soften, keeping attention on the small scene itself.

Mood: The image is mysterious, refined and warm. The dark bar setting suggests luxury and exclusivity. Smoke brings mystery and anticipation into the scene, as if we were watching an act of alchemy. The photograph conveys the joy of discovery and the intimacy of an unplanned celebration. It captures an everyday marvel and suggests that the best moments are often found on an unexpected detour.

Artwork

The Quiet Strength of Ballet

Editorial title based on the artwork description

Title recorded in the source: A Lélek Tánca

Prices in the submitted 2025 text are archival, not current offers.

Editorial translation of the submitted 2025 text

Price listed in 2025: €250. Not a current offer.

1. Work description (context)

A Lélek Tánca (Dance of the Soul) is a dramatic, symbolic portrait in which the beauty of ballet conveys persistence and emotional fire. Behind it lies a lifelong attraction to music and movement. German folk dance, hip-hop and bachata all played a part before the photographer found the purest expression of that passion in ballet. The image brings together a ballet dream that never came true and a vocation found in photography. It holds the fragility and strength of an art the photographer regards as sacred and inviolable.

2. Title, technique and philosophy

The title gives the emotional and inward meaning of movement precedence over physical form.

Technique: High-contrast black and white gives the photograph drama and a sense of timelessness. Strong studio light from above sculpts the dancer’s muscles and picks out the falling curtain of water. Its fine, broken lines suggest movement while the figure is held with precision. A fast shutter speed and carefully managed light bring two different things together: the chaotic fall of the water and a single frozen movement.

Philosophy: The work contrasts inner fire with outward struggle. The water is not only rain but the noise, difficulties and expectations of everyday life. Balancing on the tips of pointe shoes, the dancer embodies the ability to create grace and find endurance in the face of pressure. The gesture speaks of sacrifice, discipline and beauty.

3. Composition and mood

Composition: The dancer’s body and raised arms establish the vertical axis. A pose close to an arabesque and the crossing of the legs create tension. The wide sweep of fabric, still in motion, frames the body with softer horizontal lines and conveys an airy freedom. The darkness behind and the falling lines of light create the solitude of a stage.

Mood: The image feels dramatic, tender and intense. Black and white, flowing water and the dancer’s lifted expression suggest an emotional release. Dance is not entertainment here but a confession of feeling. The photograph honours determination and an unreachable dream, finding peace and strength even in the middle of a storm.

Mood: The photograph is romantic and uplifting. Even at a distance, the intimacy of the encounter comes through in the photographer’s happiness and the magic of that birthday. It is a luminous, lasting record of a priceless experience.

Farkas Tamás

Artwork

Portrait with a Blindfold

Editorial title based on the artwork description

Title recorded in the source: Ő

Prices in the submitted 2025 text are archival, not current offers.

Editorial translation of the submitted 2025 text

Price listed in 2025: €250. Not a current offer.

1. Work description (context)

Tamás Farkas’s Ő is an intimate portrait that speaks more broadly about creativity, letting go and the pressure of finding time. The sitter, described in the original as “A nagy Ő,” meaning “the one,” was reluctant to face the lens after earlier negative experiences. For this sitting a blindfold offered both a physical and a psychological separation from the surroundings, making it easier to surrender to the music and the moment.

The photograph was taken during a deeply personal performance. The sitter played their own cello without sheet music, turning towards the light as though guided by an inner sense. The light becomes a metaphor for hope, longing and painful recognition. Amid the rush of life, a passion for music can be pushed aside. The blindfolded figure facing the light carries the weight of melodies not yet played.

2. Title, technique and philosophy

The Hungarian title Ő is a third-person pronoun that does not specify gender. It refers to a particular person, while leaving the viewer room to think about their own unspoken desires.

Technique: The play of light and shadow gives the picture emotional depth. A carefully chosen depth of field appears to keep the figure in focus and blur the setting, drawing attention to the sitter’s inner world. A strong, directed light seems perceptible even through the blindfold; it reveals contours and textures without dispelling the mystery. The source reads the restrained or monochrome palette as another way of drawing attention to feeling.

Philosophy: The blindfold has two meanings. It protects the sitter from outside expectations and makes it possible to let go. It also stands for inner sight and the intuition behind direct artistic expression. Music played without a score and out of sight becomes an image of instinctive creativity. Turning towards the light with sight blocked evokes hopes and desires that remain alive even in a crowded life. The picture looks for self-acceptance and inward freedom within external limits. In the quiet away from outside noise, one may hear something true in oneself.

3. Composition and mood

The sitter is at the centre, strengthening the connection with the viewer. The cello is not a prop but an extension of the person holding it. The expression, the posture, the position of the hands and the closeness to the instrument convey feeling even with the eyes covered. An apparently tight crop keeps attention on the sitter’s inner world.

The photograph is melancholy and transcendent at once. The blindfolded figure appears vulnerable and strong. Releasing the music and turning towards the light make a moment of inward freedom. The atmosphere is dreamlike and meditative, as though the sound had taken visible form. The work invites reflection on passions we have put aside, the restorative power of art and how we might find light within the most crowded parts of life.

In the Quiet of the Cello

Editorial title based on the artwork description

Title recorded in the source: Én

Prices in the submitted 2025 text are archival, not current offers.

Editorial translation of the submitted 2025 text

Price listed in 2025: €250. Not a current offer.

1. Work description (context)

Én (Self) gives visual form to a personal story. It is the first work the artist made in collaboration with a partner. It captures a moment of breaking away from the noise of demanding days, when the deep, resonant sound of the cello offers refuge from everyday bustle. The self-portrait-like image evokes the solitude, immersion and inner peace music can bring. In its many forms, music helps the artist leave the rush of the outside world and find a way back to the self. Silence and sound, light and shadow lead the viewer into an inner world where the soul finds rest.

2. Title, technique and philosophy

Én means “I”. Here it is more than a pronoun: it emphasises the personal, introspective character of a moment when music makes it possible to retreat into an inner space.

Technique: The lighting is deliberately shaped. The main light seems to fall on the cello and partly on the subject, while the background sinks into darkness. This chiaroscuro not only sets off the subject but evokes the noise of the world fading away. A deliberately shallow depth of field keeps the instrument and the subject’s hand or face sharp while the surroundings blur. Black and white strips away the visual noise of colour, giving the image a clear, timeless character and turning attention towards feeling.

Philosophy: The photograph reflects on music’s restorative power and the refuge it offers. The cello becomes a source of quiet, focus and moments of feeling true to oneself.
