01 / Exhibition story

Community photography exhibition · 2025

The Image of My Life

A photographer creates thousands of pictures over a lifetime. What happens when they are asked to choose the one image that carries the greatest personal meaning? The Image of My Life explored that question.

Presented by the Vienna Hungarian School Photography Club, the exhibition brought together twenty photographs attributed to fifteen Hungarian photographers at CITYgalleryVIENNA. Different life paths and points of view converged around personal images.

The exhibition sought to open a conversation between artist and viewer, connecting Hungarian photographers living in different countries.

1. Work description (context)

Wild is the Wind was made at a particular moment and place. Around Christmas 2024, the Danube was high and the groundwater in the Csallóköz region had risen unusually far. At the flooded gravel-pit lake at Lakszakállas, wind and water gave the landscape a surreal atmosphere. The photographer set out not to document the event but to interpret it and catch the unrestrained energy of the elements.

The original gives the work’s location and year as Csallóköz, 2024. The title conveys the image’s energy and the photographer’s choice to give up some control deliberately.

2. Technique and philosophy

The photographer used redscale film, loaded back to front. Its base acts as a colour filter, giving the view deep, hot reds and oranges. The photograph was taken with a homemade pinhole camera, or camera obscura, with a curved back that widens the perspective. The exposure of about 40 seconds records the action of water, light and wind slowly, softening the boundary of an instant.

The pinhole camera and redscale film are a protest against digital perfection. Working by hand and entirely in analogue, the photographer lets light and chemistry, chance and intuition shape the image together. The surreal result comes from the interaction between intention and technique.

3. Composition and mood

With the pinhole camera’s curved back, the space stretches at the edges and seems to wrap around the viewer, making the landscape feel organic and dreamlike.

The long exposure softens the water and movement in the sky into painterly blur. The landscape becomes a single glowing vision. The redscale colours act as an emotional filter, conveying a melancholy that is both unsettling and peaceful, and the anxiety and humility that nature’s power can evoke. The picture becomes a metaphor for the beauty and transience of the world.

A Wild is the Wind című alkotás egy meghatározott idő és tér metszéspontjában született. A 2024-es karácsonyi időszakot a Duna magas vízszintje és a Csallóköz térségében extrém módon megemelkedett talajvíz jellemezte. 2. Technika és Filozófia:

A kép egyediségét a Redscale film használata adja, melyet szándékosan fordítva fűztek be. Ezáltal a film hordozórétege effektív színszűrőként működik, a valóságot forró, mélyvörös és narancs tónusokra transzformálva. A felvétel egy saját készítésű lyukkamerával készült (camera obscura), amelynek görbített hátfala tudatosan kiszélesíti a perspektívát. A körülbelül 40 másodperces hosszú expozíció a víz, a fény és a szél mozgásának lassú, meditatív regisztrációja, eltörölve a pillanat éles határát.
A lyukkamera és a Redscale film használata a digitális tökéletesség elleni kiáltás. Az alkotó a tisztán analóg, kézműves folyamatban hisz, ahol a kép a kémia és a fény találkozásából, a véletlen és az intuíció együttállásából születik meg, így a szürreális eredmény a szándék és a technika szoros együttműködését hirdeti.

3. Kompozíció és Hangulat:

A görbített hátfalú lyukkamera következtében a kompozíció peremén a tér vizuálisan megnyúlik, szinte körülöleli a nézőt, ami felerősíti a táj organikus, álomszerű érzetét.
A 40 másodperces záridő a vizet és az égi mozgást finom, festői elmosódássá oldja, szinte beletörölve a tájat egyetlen nagy, lángoló vízióba. A Redscale tónusok nem csupán színek, hanem érzelmi szűrők: fenyegető, mégis békés melankóliát, a természet hatalmából fakadó szorongást és alázatot sugároznak. A kép a gyönyörű, ám múlandó világ metaforájaként hat.

2. Title: Toscana Dream (Val d’Orcia, 2016)

3. Technique and philosophy: This photograph is the memory of a journey both personal and experimental. I took it in Tuscany in 2016, in the beautiful Orcia valley, on my first holiday with my wife.

I deliberately chose expired Ektachrome 200 slide film. Cross-processing gave the image its distinctive character: the film, designed for the E-6 process, was developed in C-41 chemistry.

The cross-processing produced strong, distinct grain and an unusual colour shift. The rows of Tuscan cypresses took on unexpected, dreamlike tones, heightening the landscape’s melancholy, surreal atmosphere.

4. Composition and mood: A classic view of the Orcia valley creates a dramatic contrast. An even, timeless line of trees leads the eye into the distance, while the painterly cloud formations filling the sky are essential to the effect. The photograph does not merely document the scene; it rewrites the memory. Cross-processing becomes a visual encoding of nostalgia, adding a richer emotional layer to reality.

About the artist

Editorial translation of the submitted 2025 text

Stopping time, the analogue way

I have been recording reality since 1983. I am one of those “analogue dinosaurs” who still work with analogue cameras and classic film processes, whether with negatives or slides.

Developing a black-and-white negative is a sacred ritual for me. There is no deeper creative experience than watching a work come into being by my own hands, as chemistry and light meet in the developing tank. This hands-on process is photography in its original, essential form. Every time I develop a film it is a different experience.

One particular advantage of analogue storage is that the images can be viewed without a digital device, are easy to keep over the long term and can be reproduced without difficulty. You can even enjoy a slide by candlelight.

The primacy of the moment

The work begins in the viewfinder. I compose the image before making the exposure, through the interplay of subjects, light and shadow. Only when I have fully formed the image in my mind do I release the shutter. The camera is a tool for looking deliberately, not for correcting things later.

I make my photographs without digital manipulation. A photograph should be a photograph, not an exercise in Photoshop acrobatics, moving sliders back and forth or cutting and pasting different backgrounds. In my view, that takes neither a photographer’s eye nor talent. I would call this sort of image-making digital montage rather than photography.

Mission

The world is a wonder in itself. My artistic mission is to preserve as many remarkable moments as I can for those who come after me, seen from my own point of view. I make photographs as a monument against the passing of time: breaths held still before a moment slips away like a drop of rain in a downpour.

1. Work description (concept and context)

Éljen a Dirndl (Long Live the Dirndl) expresses respect for traditional dress, femininity and enduring elegance. HajNika’s photograph emphasises the symbolic power of the dirndl. More than a garment, it is a cultural symbol that can bring out the wearer’s confidence and quiet charm. For the photographer, the work celebrates the feeling that anyone wearing it can feel entirely at ease in her own femininity.

2. Title, technique and philosophy

The title is a declaration against passing fashions. As a cultural icon, the dirndl stands for something lasting in a world where trends change quickly.

HajNika values quality, honesty and character. The technique brings out the contrasts between materials such as silk, velvet and cloth. The light is soft but deliberate, with the emphasis on keeping an authentic look. Fine folds, the fitted bodice and the lightness of the apron (Schürze) form a visual conversation in which traditional dress frames a contemporary wearer.

3. Composition and mood

The sitter is placed centrally and gently separated from the surroundings. The symmetrical bodice (Mieder) and the carefully framed neckline draw the eye. The pose conveys dignity, poise and confidence.

The atmosphere is romantic, nostalgic and uplifting. The source suggests deep red and white tones, bringing a sense of gentle charm. The picture proposes that wearing a dirndl is not simply an echo of the past but an expression of self-possession and cultural identity. It celebrates the beauty and dignity the artist sees in femininity.

1. Work description (context and vision)

In Feuer und Flamme, HajNika finds universal feelings in the intimate sight of a burning candle. The photograph records more than candlelight. It gives shape to an inner vision of love and peace in the dance of a flame. A glowing heart seems to merge with the candle, and a dove rises towards the light. Together they suggest hope, purity and feelings that endure. The image invites reflection, turning an outward light into a mirror of the inner world.

2. Title, technique and philosophy

The German title literally means fire and flame. As an expression it also conveys enthusiasm or passion for something.

Technique: Soft focus and careful lighting set the image apart. The bokeh, or softly blurred spots of light behind the candle, dissolves the surrounding space and directs attention to the central form and its light. The unobtrusive digital post-processing integrates the symbolic heart and dove without disrupting the atmosphere.

Philosophy: The candle carries a meaning beyond the object itself. Its flame suggests life, hope and transience; the heart and dove express love and peace. For HajNika, light can reveal emotional and spiritual layers beyond visible reality.

3. Composition and mood

The candle is placed at the centre, giving the symbol particular force. It is lit from below with a halo-like glow, as if shining from within. A heart carved into the candle and a dove that seems to rise from the flame make a still scene feel alive. Details that at first look subtle give the image several layers of meaning.

Warm golds suggest calm and the comfort of home. The dark, blurred background brings the candle’s radiance into focus and makes the scene meditative and peaceful. The viewer can almost feel the heat and pulse of the flame. The work asks us to slow down and look in the light for the things in ourselves that endure.

Léna Erdős

Artwork

Exhibited photograph 06

Editorial translation of the submitted 2025 text

1. Work description (context)

Remény – Hoffnung – Hope brings together the spiritual and emotional concerns in Léna Erdős’s art. The image answers what the artist calls a summons from the soul. It is not a record of external reality, but a way of expressing an inner world, possibility and a fascination with colour. For Erdős, what comes from the heart and soul can become a work of art. This picture explores hope through form and atmosphere, carrying its message in three languages.

2. Title, technique and philosophy

The trilingual title expresses the artist’s conviction that the feeling can be shared across languages and cultures.

Technique: Colour and light play a central role. A long exposure or intentional camera movement (ICM) may have dissolved the scene into painterly patches. Soft edges and spreading light hold the feeling of a moment rather than its physical detail. I was born in Bratislava, Slovakia, but have lived in Vienna since 1996. It is the home I chose, and it keeps inspiring me.

The world of photography really opened up to me in September 2024. I enrolled in a photography course at the Hungarian School in Vienna (BMI) with Norbert Bánhalmi. He taught me the basics and encouraged me to develop an artistic way of seeing.

The joy of discovery

Through the lens I learned to pay close attention to the interplay of light and shadow and to the subtleties of composition. I enjoy searching for the right angle, discovering symmetry and uncovering messages hidden in everyday moments. I want to see more clearly the beauty and small details we so often pass by.

Photography is a way to unwind and meditate. It takes me out of the everyday and into a different world, where I learn new techniques and begin to think differently. I am grateful to have found a passion that brings me joy and a community I value through the course.

“My camera is the key to a world where beauty lies in the details.”

Pozsonyban, Szlovákiában születtem, de 1996 óta élek Bécsben, a választott hazámban, amely folyamatosan inspirál.

A fotográfia világa 2024 szeptemberében nyílt meg előttem igazán. Ekkor kezdtem el a Bécsi Magyar Iskola (BMI) fotótanfolyamát Bánhalmi Norbert vezetésével, aki nem csupán az alapokat adta át, hanem a művészi látásmód kialakítására is ösztönöz.

A Felfedezés Öröme
A lencsén keresztül megtanultam tudatosan figyelni a fény és árnyék játékára, a kompozíció finomságaira. Minden alkalommal örömöt jelent számomra a legmegfelelőbb látószög keresése, a szimmetria felfedezése és a hétköznapi pillanatokba rejtett üzenetek kibontása. Célom, hogy egyre élesebben lássam a szépséget és az apró részleteket, amelyek mellett oly sokszor elmegyünk.

A fotózás számomra egyben kikapcsolódás és meditáció. Segít kilépni a hétköznapokból, és egy másik világba kalauzol, ahol új technikákat sajátítok el és másképp kezdek gondolkodni. Hálás vagyok, hogy felfedeztem magamnak ezt a szenvedélyt, amely örömmel tölt el, és értékes közösséget is adott a tanfolyam révén.

"A fényképezőgépem a kulcs ahhoz a világhoz, ahol a szépség a részletekben rejlik."
