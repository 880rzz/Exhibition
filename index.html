01 / Premise

We spent a year looking through the eyes of others. This exhibition is about how we see.

Third year · third major group exhibition

A club became a community.

VIPACH grew out of the BMI Fotoklub in Vienna. In its third year, the community entered a new chapter: international, open and connected by photography.

The change of name was more than a rebrand. Vienna Photo Art & Creative Hub describes what the group had already become: a place where people with different backgrounds, levels of experience and visual languages meet, learn and create together. English became the common communication language, while the Hungarian roots remain part of the story.

This is our third major group exhibition in Vienna. It closes another intense learning chapter — not with a single prescribed style, but with many individual ways of seeing.

The name

A word about becoming.

VIPACH is the acronym for Vienna Photo Art & Creative Hub. It also carries an older linguistic echo that became part of our identity: on the VIPACH story page, the Sanskrit word vipach is described as “to ripen”, “to develop”, “to come to fruition”.

That is close to how we think about photography: not as a race for technical perfection, but as a process in which attention, practice and personal vision slowly mature.

The learning year

The great photographers.

Month by month, art historian Dávid Balogh introduced us to photographers who changed the visual language of their time.

We did not stop at style, equipment or technique. We asked what shaped them, what they chose to notice, and why their images still guide photographers today. In that process we also discovered how deeply Hungarian-born photographers helped shape the history of the medium.

04 / The turn

SPONTÁN

Creativity asks us to loosen control — and allow the process itself to lead.
In the spirit of Mihály Csíkszentmihályi

At first, the exhibition had another working title: In the footsteps of the great photographers. It was meant as a tribute to the masters whose lives and images accompanied us throughout the year.

Then the exhibition started to change. New photographers joined — people who had not travelled the whole learning path with us, but were driven by the same curiosity. The show found its own direction.

A sudden idea. A gesture caught by chance. A light that disappears a second later. Something ordinary in which perhaps only one person sees beauty. These are the moments on the walls.
