01 / Starting point

For a year, we paid attention to how others saw. Now we want to show what we notice.

Third year · third group exhibition

It started with five people. It became a community.

In autumn 2024 we were still meeting as BMI Fotoklub Wien. We took photographs, looked at them together and talked about why some images stayed with us.

At the beginning of 2026, that community became VIPACH — Vienna Photo Art & Creative Hub. More people joined from different backgrounds, so English naturally became our common language. The Hungarian roots are still part of who we are.

SPONTANEOUS is our third group exhibition in Vienna. We are not trying to show one shared style. What interests us is almost the opposite: how differently people can look at the same world.

The name

A name about something slowly coming into its own.

VIPACH stands for Vienna Photo Art & Creative Hub. When we chose the name, another meaning also felt close to us: in our own story, vipach is connected with ripening, developing and coming to fruition.

That is also close to how we think about photography. The goal is not to make every image technically perfect. It is to learn to see more clearly, and over time understand what genuinely matters to us.

04 / The turn

SPONTANEOUS

Sometimes something interesting happens when, for a moment, we stop trying to control it.
In the spirit of Mihály Csíkszentmihályi

Our first working title was In the footsteps of the great photographers. It made sense. Their images and stories had been with us throughout the year.

Then the community changed. New people arrived with different experience and a different way of looking. At some point it became clear that we did not want to follow anyone’s footsteps. We had looked carefully at what they saw. Now we wanted to show what we see.

SPONTANEOUS is therefore not built around one subject. It is about the moments that make us stop: a light, a gesture, an odd situation, something ordinary that perhaps only one person notices.

About the work

At the Pflasterspektakel in Linz, an aerial acrobat swings between three poles using a shimmering white fabric. Music accompanies her powerful movements, which she performs with remarkable lightness. Her controlled turns high above the ground hold the audience’s attention.

About the artist

This year, alongside landscapes, I have been drawn to scenes of city life and portrait photography. The fleeting moments of a busy city fascinate me as a counterpoint to the calm of nature.

Studying the techniques of renowned photographers with the Vienna photography school group was a rewarding experience. After the theoretical sessions, the real challenge was recognising parallels in the movement of the city. The magic of a moment continues to amaze me. Photography is both an adventure and a challenge, and it draws me in again and again.

Exhibitors · 03

loksi67

Tallinn Connection

6 July 2026

About the work

Few European capitals are as pleasantly surprising as Tallinn. Behind its medieval walls are winding lanes, historic merchants’ houses and lively squares. A few minutes away, former industrial districts are becoming home to restaurants, design shops and contemporary office buildings. The mix of history and innovation makes the city particularly compelling.

For this setting, I wanted to try a distinctive film. I loaded a roll of Harmann Switch Azure 125 into my analogue Canon EOS 5 and was curious about the result. The film turns blues orange, vivid yellows azure and reds shades of purple and blue. I was not disappointed: the colours are so intense that many people assume they were created in Photoshop. Yet this unusual view of the Swedbank office tower was made without digital post-production.

About the artist

My name is Zoltan Vass. I have been recording the world with my cameras since 1983. I count myself among the “analogue dinosaurs”: I still work with film, both negatives and slides, in 35mm and medium format. Cross-processing, redscale and camera obscura are part of my everyday practice.

For me, everything begins with the moment. Subjects are there in the street; you need to notice them and preserve them on film. Even the smallest things have extraordinary potential. I compose each photograph in the viewfinder, working with subject, light and shadow before releasing the shutter. The camera is a way of seeing deliberately, not a tool for later correction.

I make my photographs without digital editing. I believe a photograph should remain a photograph. Extensive changes in Photoshop, substituted backgrounds and other transformations are, to me, digital montage rather than photography. I do not see the same sensitivity or photographic skill in that approach.

The world is a wonder. I want to preserve as many remarkable moments as possible from my own perspective, as a record for those who come after us. Through photographs, I try to hold something against the passage of time before these moments disappear, like tears in rain.
