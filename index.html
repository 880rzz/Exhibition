01 / Starting point

For a year, we paid attention to how others saw. Now we want to show what we notice.

Third year · third group exhibition

It started with five people. It became a community.

In autumn 2024 we were still meeting as BMI Fotoklub Wien. We took photographs, looked at them together and talked about why some images stayed with us.

The photography club operates as a community club of the Central Association of Hungarian Associations and Organisations in Austria, within the Vienna Hungarian School (Bécsi Magyar Iskola). This is where our shared learning and photographic encounters found a home.

At the beginning of 2026, that community became VIPACH — Vienna Photo Art & Creative Hub. More people joined from different backgrounds, so English naturally became our common language. The Hungarian roots are still part of who we are.

SPONTANEOUS is our third group exhibition in Vienna. We are not trying to show one shared style. What interests us is almost the opposite: how differently people can look at the same world.

The name

A name about something slowly coming into its own.

VIPACH stands for Vienna Photo Art & Creative Hub. When we chose the name, another meaning also felt close to us: in our own story, vipach is connected with ripening, developing and coming to fruition.

That is also close to how we think about photography. The goal is not to make every image technically perfect. It is to learn to see more clearly, and over time understand what genuinely matters to us.

04 / The turn

SPONTANEOUS

Sometimes something interesting happens when, for a moment, we stop trying to control it.
In the spirit of Mihály Csíkszentmihályi

Our first working title was In the footsteps of the great photographers. It made sense. Their images and stories had been with us throughout the year.

Then the community changed. New people arrived with different experience and a different way of looking. At some point it became clear that we did not want to follow anyone’s footsteps. We had looked carefully at what they saw. Now we wanted to show what we see.

SPONTANEOUS is therefore not built around one subject. It is about the moments that make us stop: a light, a gesture, an odd situation, something ordinary that perhaps only one person notices.

At the Pflasterspektakel in Linz, an aerial acrobat swings between three poles using a shimmering white fabric. Music accompanies her powerful movements, which she performs with remarkable lightness. Her controlled turns high above the ground hold the audience’s attention.

About the artist

Exhibitors · 03

loksi67

Tallinn Connection

Price €299

6 July 2026

About the work

Few European capitals are as pleasantly surprising as Tallinn. Behind its medieval walls are winding lanes, historic merchants’ houses and lively squares. A few minutes away, former industrial districts are becoming home to restaurants, design shops and contemporary office buildings. The mix of history and innovation makes the city particularly compelling.

For this setting, I wanted to try a distinctive film. I loaded a roll of Harmann Switch Azure 125 into my analogue Canon EOS 5 and was curious about the result. The film turns blues orange, vivid yellows azure and reds shades of purple and blue. I was not disappointed: the colours are so intense that many people assume they were created in Photoshop. Yet this unusual view of the Swedbank office tower was made without digital post-production.

Another work

Kitzbüheler Horn

Price €299

20 September 2026

Kitzbühel stays in the memory: mountain ranges, wide valleys and the feeling of summer. From Ehrenbachhöhe, the view towards Kitzbüheler Horn is unforgettable. The Hahnenkamm mountain station lies in the foreground, with the mountains around Lofer and Hochkönig beyond. I could spend hours looking at this panorama. The moment brought to mind the sense of freedom in Nina Simone’s “Feeling Good”.

About the artist

My name is Zoltan Vass. I have been recording the world with my cameras since 1983. I count myself among the “analogue dinosaurs”: I still work with film, both negatives and slides, in 35mm and medium format. After more than four decades of analogue photography, I have also started exploring digital cameras to compare the two approaches. Cross-processing, redscale and camera obscura are part of my everyday practice.

For me, everything begins with the moment. Subjects are there in the street; you need to notice them and preserve them on film. Even the smallest things have extraordinary potential. I compose each photograph in the viewfinder, working with subject, light and shadow before releasing the shutter. The camera is a way of seeing deliberately, not a tool for later correction.

I make my photographs without digital editing. I believe a photograph should remain a photograph. Extensive changes in Photoshop, substituted backgrounds and other transformations are, to me, digital montage rather than photography. I do not see the same sensitivity or photographic skill in that approach.

The world is a wonder. I want to preserve as many remarkable moments as possible from my own perspective, as a record for those who come after us. Through photographs, I try to hold something against the passage of time before these moments disappear, like tears in rain.

Exhibitors · 04

Adrian Ferenczik

The Art of the Unseen

Price €290

4 July 2026

About the work

Sometimes the most striking photographs appear close to home. While watering flowers, I noticed a small European garden spider (Araneus diadematus) that had built an unexpectedly large web among them. The spider was barely two centimetres long, but its web revealed a complex arrangement of fine lines.

I had already become interested in photographing the intricate structures of the human iris. With a macro lens, I applied that same attention to the spider and discovered textures and patterns that could easily go unnoticed. The photograph reminds me that we do not always need to go far to find something extraordinary; sometimes we only need to look more closely.

Another work

Between Serenity and Chaos

Price €290

17 August 2026

During a holiday in Rovinj, Croatia, I was walking through the old town as sunset and an approaching storm met over the sea. A narrow opening between the dark clouds and the horizon let the last sunlight colour the sky pink, violet and orange.

The colours looked almost unreal, and I knew the scene could disappear within minutes. I took out my camera to preserve it. Some of the most meaningful photographs happen without planning, when nature offers a moment and we have only time to notice it.

About the artist

Photography allows me to preserve what makes a moment or a person unique. Based in Lower Austria, I work in two different but equally meaningful fields: wedding and event photography, and iris photography. At celebrations, I look for spontaneous emotion and small stories, so that people can return to those feelings long after the day has passed.

Iris photography opens another perspective. Every iris has its own colours, patterns and fine details; viewed close up, something deeply personal becomes visually compelling. Whether I am documenting an event or photographing an eye, my goal is to reveal the individuality and beauty of each subject.

Friends & Bresson

19 September 2025

About the work

This photograph was taken in Vienna in September 2025, in the style of Henri Cartier-Bresson, when we visited an exhibition of his work. After the exhibition, my son and his friend leaned against an illuminated wall. I was so moved by the image’s symmetry, geometry and many layers that I am delighted to exhibit it here.

This exhibited work is about how art does not end at the museum walls. When we leave an exhibition, the way we see changes: the world itself becomes a photograph.

In the foreground, the two boys appear almost as silhouettes against the illuminated installation. Their postures and the way they pause with their scooters create the rhythmic balance that Cartier-Bresson called the “decisive moment”.

One of my favourite photographs.

Balogh Dávid

Időtlen elegancia – hommage à Edward Steichen

Price €249

18 October 2026

About the work

This photograph is a tribute to the work of Edward Steichen. I was inspired by a portrait he made for Vogue in 1935 of the actress Mary Heberden wearing an elegant silk evening gown.

The turned pose, the line of the shoulders, the prominent bow and the painterly backlighting recall the earlier composition without copying it. The person in my photograph is my partner, Garas Médea. Her three-quarter profile, the texture of the dress and the vertical rhythm of the pearls along her back evoke fashion photography of the 1930s. Rather than reproducing Steichen’s cooler, more classical approach, I wanted the image to feel warmer, more intimate and personal.

About the artist

I studied art history at the Faculty of Humanities of Eötvös Loránd University (ELTE), where my studies included Hungarian and international photographic history. I work as director of the Németh Kálmán Memorial House and research the local history of Fót from historical, ethnographic and art-historical perspectives. Over the years I have curated and opened photography-history exhibitions in Hungary and abroad, including New York and Vienna.

As an educator at the Bécsi Magyar Iskola, I explore photographers and historical periods through their context, techniques, emotional impact and artistic significance, with particular attention to the relationship between painting and photography. At the photo club, I bring an art-historical perspective to image analysis. My aim is to help participants recognise connections between movements, genres and their times, and develop a more conscious visual language alongside their technical skills.

KatiFoto

Feminine Grace (Női báj)

Price €249

22 February 2026

About the work

Feminine Grace — in the photographic tradition of Horst P. Horst. Feminine beauty lies not only in perfect forms, but also in gestures, presence and subtle details.

The work explores the elegance and sensuality of femininity while deliberately moving beyond conventional depictions of beauty. Movement, posture and the act of stepping express both vulnerability and strength, as well as dignity. Femininity finds its fullest expression not in flawlessness but in individuality, authenticity and presence.

Drawing on the iconic fashion photography of Horst P. Horst, the image brings classical elegance together with a contemporary, personal perspective. Suggestion, considered framing and contrasting light leave room for the viewer's imagination.

Feminine grace does not lie in perfection, but in the unique beauty of every woman following her own path.

About the artist

For me, photography is more than preserving moments. It is a way to tell stories, convey emotions and reveal beauty that often goes unnoticed in everyday life.

I am especially inspired by people, genuine emotions and natural moments. I aim to create images that are more than memories: images that convey feelings.

I work around Vienna and Győr, while remaining eager to explore new places and stories with my camera. I believe a truly good photograph shows not only what we see, but also what we feel.

Tóth Manó

Pier on the Prairie

Price €249

10 May 2026

About the work

After a two-day train journey, I arrived in Chicago from Portland. A dream of mine came true when, in May 2026, I was able to admire the Rocky Mountains from the panoramic carriage of an Amtrak train. During the long journey, the train wound its way through high mountains and desolate plains. Empty, uninhabited expanses and nature stretched as far as the eye could see.

Upon arriving in Chicago, the horizontal space suddenly narrowed, and I found myself among soaring skyscrapers. The sprawling, enormous scale of the prairie gave way to the ostentatious, upward-reaching monumentality of the “Paris of the Prairie”. All of this was embraced by the seemingly endless waters of Lake Michigan.

Yet the people in the big city were just as approachable and open towards me as those I had spoken with on the train or at stations along the way. I felt that at Navy Pier, the prairie, the water, the sky and the people came together.

In this photograph, I captured that experience: the immensity, the open space, the meeting of horizontal and vertical lines, the openness, and the city, lake and people bathed in sparkling spring sunshine.

I am offering a €250 donation for the photograph

My experiences in America could not have been the same if I had not met those endlessly kind, friendly and generous people who welcomed me, helped me and shared their stories with me. Among them, Emily and Alex stand out; I met them at a professional conference in Portland. Their son, Otto, loved LEGO, Pokémon and anime. He fought neuroblastoma, one of the severe forms of childhood cancer, for ten years. Sadly, Otto passed away recently at the age of fifteen.

I would like to ask the future owner of the photograph to support Otto’s family with a donation through their GoFundMe page. In this way, I hope to help Emily and Alex receive support during this difficult time and to keep Otto’s memory alive.

About the artist

I love the feeling of looking through a camera viewfinder and watching everything change. New compositions, light and perspectives emerge, and even the most ordinary things can become magical. Beyond photography, I am passionate about tea, water sports, cycling and chocolate.

Gábor

A tale of two parts

Price €249

9 February 2022

About the work

There is something profoundly humbling about witnessing the majestic landscapes of Antarctica, where nature creates sculptures of unimaginable beauty, shaped by time, ice, and the relentless forces of the Weddell Sea.

I captured this moment from a small boat, shortly after sunset, surrounded by the silence and vastness of this extraordinary wilderness.

What struck me most was the story these two magnificent icebergs seemed to tell. Once part of the same formation, they had been torn apart by the forces of nature, drifting away from one another into the icy waters.

Yet, despite the distance separating them, their shapes reveal an unmistakable connection. Like two pieces of a puzzle, they still carry the memory of having once been whole.

To me, this photograph is about more than the breathtaking beauty of Antarctica. It is a reflection on connection, separation, and belonging. A reminder that even when life pulls things apart, their shared origins remain, sometimes so clearly that no distance can erase them.

Two icebergs. One origin. Separated by distance, but forever connected.

About the work

The surface of the water does more than separate: it forms an invisible bridge between two distinct worlds. Above it, a rocky shoreline and the silhouettes of onlookers meet the clear, turquoise stillness below, where a solitary seal swims through the light.

Bánhalmi Norbert

Ébredés – az új kezdet

Price €499

Photographed in autumn 2010 · reimagined in 2017

About the work

The original photograph was made in autumn 2010. In 2017, Norbert Bánhalmi reimagined it through a double-exposure process, creating the image associated with the cover of his book Ébredés – az Új kezdet! (Awakening – A New Beginning). The overlapping visual layers evoke memory, transformation and the possibility of beginning again.

About the book

Published in 2017, Ébredés – az Új kezdet! brings together the personal stories of twelve women living after gynaecological cancer, told through fifteen photographs and the participants’ own words. Developed with the involvement of Dr Magdolna Dank, Erika Elek and Imre Csernus, the book approaches body image, acceptance and human dignity with care. ISBN 978-963-12-8663-2.

About the book

About the artist

Norbert Bánhalmi has worked in photography since 1999. Based between Vienna and Budapest, he works across portraiture, documentary photography and artistic projects, with attention to presence, identity and personal stories. BANHALMI ART brings together his exhibitions, books and independent bodies of work.
