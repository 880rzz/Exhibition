Original event listing

01 / Exhibition story

Twenty photographs. Personal perspectives. One shared encounter.

Community photography exhibition · 2025

As I Like It Austria

As I Like It Austria explored the meeting of Hungarian and Austrian cultural experiences through photography. Members of the Vienna Hungarian School Photography Club looked for beauty, stillness and human connection in everyday life.

The photographs brought together individual ways of seeing: the city, its architecture and light, the intimacy of ordinary moments and questions of identity.

The exhibition invited visitors not simply to look but to observe more closely, finding meaning in the moments a camera can hold.

Liz

Artwork

Exhibited photograph 18

Prices in the submitted 2025 text are archival, not current offers.

Editorial translation of the submitted 2025 text

Price listed in 2025: €250. Not a current offer.

1. Work description (context)

Kisses from Vienna finds an unexpected encounter and a feeling of love in the lights of the Austrian capital at night. The photographer made it on an ordinary tram ride home from work. An illuminated word, “Kuss” (“kiss”), on the façade of the Vienna State Opera suddenly brought Gustav Klimt’s The Kiss to mind. It became an image of the photographer’s attachment to Vienna and a growing passion for photography. An ordinary city journey meets the power of a work of art.

2. Title, technique and philosophy

Light as muse, the window as frame.

Technique: The photograph makes the most of limited conditions. Framed by a tram window and softened by movement, the opera house moves away from sharp description and into a fluid, dreamlike scene. Lights on the façade, the glowing “Kuss” and star-like spots seen through the glass make the image expressive. The available, low evening light contributes to its depth and dark, rich tones.

Philosophy: The word and its echo of Klimt are a cultural tribute. The work suggests that love and art can find us at any time, even on a rushed weekday. Vienna becomes both the muse and the setting for a love story, connecting a personal experience with the golden, romantic inheritance of Viennese Art Nouveau.

3. Composition

The tram window confines the view and makes it feel intimate. The photograph is organised around the imposing opera house, blurred by light, with the golden word “Kuss” at its centre. The word catches the eye like a sudden recognition.

4. Mood

The image is romantic, surprising and golden. Evening light gives it warmth and the intimacy of a memory. It evokes the excitement of discovering love, as though all the lights in the world had gathered into one message. Klimt-like gold against dark tones gives a fleeting moment the quality of a dream. The photograph is a sweet Viennese kiss made visible.

Artwork

Editorial translation of the submitted 2025 text

1. Work description (context)

A fények és dallamok tánca Bécsben (The Dance of Lights and Melodies in Vienna) is a photomontage and a love letter to the city. The photographer deliberately layered two favourite photographs to share a personal experience. Vienna appears as both a modern Babylon and an imperial city, where music, culture, art, elegance and light are part of ordinary life. The warmth of musicians in underpasses and the brightness of the night keep surprising and inspiring the artist.

2. Title, technique and philosophy

The title makes music a visual metaphor for life in the city.

Technique: Two distinct photographs are layered into a scene that is surreal but holds together. The translucent layers suggest the ways Vienna’s worlds can overlap, bringing elegance into contact with modern life. The lights do more than illuminate what is shown: they are part of the composition, like a melody made visible. Rich, bright colours bring out the energy of the city.

Philosophy: Vienna changes without losing its roots. The montage puts the city itself in conversation with the artist’s private, emotional image of it. The dance of light and melody suggests recurring rhythms, a shifting balance and art that can be found around any corner.

3. Composition and mood

Composition: Layering gives the picture depth. The clock tower of Vienna’s Rathaus meets the lights of the modern city at night, and a street musician playing in a busy underpass is woven into the view. Vertical and horizontal elements keep the eye moving between details.

Mood: The work is romantic, nostalgic and modern at once. The lights make the darkness feel warm and festive; the imagined melodies convey openness and welcome. The photograph invites the viewer into a city where old and new, music and light, classical elegance and contemporary energy are inseparable. It expresses affection for a place that continues to enrich the photographer’s life.

Editorial translation of the submitted 2025 text

1. Work description (context)

This photograph reflects on the constant movement and encounters of city life. It was made at Schottentor, beside the University of Vienna’s main building. The original text describes it as a meeting point of 13 public transport lines and a crossing point for hundreds of thousands of people each day. The photographer looks for the individual stories behind the system: rushing, waiting, setting out. The city offers work, study, a meeting with friends and the chance to get away from routine.

2. Title, technique and philosophy

A nagyváros mozgásában (Wien bewegt) refers to both the physical and emotional movement of the city.

Technique: The image may have been made with a long exposure to blur movement. People hurrying to work or meeting a friend appear as faint lines or bands of energy rather than distinct figures. The flow of life and the passage of time become the subject. Sharp architectural elements stand against the blur of the crowd, contrasting the still structure with the traffic that passes through it.

Philosophy: The photograph meditates on movement and freedom. An organised transport network makes different ways of living possible, whether the next destination is work or a café. People move within a shared system while following different paths.

3. Composition and mood

Composition: The stable horizontal and vertical lines of the Schottentor and university architecture give the picture its structure. In the lower part, a stream of people dissolves into blurred lines. The apparent permanence of the built environment is set against the transience of a human moment.

Mood: The photograph is busy but contemplative. Though it is about rushing and traffic, the long exposure lends it stillness. The lights, especially if the scene was made at night as the source suggests, give the place warmth and vitality. The city becomes full of possibility rather than cold or impersonal. The scene seems to turn into liquid time.

Editorial translation of the submitted 2025 text

Price listed in 2025: €250. Not a current offer.

1. Work description (context)

Utazás (Journey) shows the area around Wien Westbahnhof, an important transport hub, but it is more than a record of the place. It sets the hard monumentality of modern architecture against the restless force of nature. The photographer’s words run through the picture: “Every Journey is a start of becoming something new.” The station entrance gives the physical journey a frame. The flock rising above it suggests freedom, change and the impulse to keep moving.

2. Title, technique and philosophy

The title refers as much to personal change as to physical travel.

Technique: Monochrome, sepia-like tones lend the modern building a sense of time and nostalgia, bringing an older Vienna into conversation with contemporary architecture. A deep depth of field renders both the foreground structure and the birds clearly. Looking upwards makes the building more imposing and opens up the sky.

Philosophy: The station, with its fixed times and destinations, contrasts with the instinctive, apparently chaotic freedom of the birds. Their flight evokes the desire to renew oneself and move towards the unknown. Every journey is a beginning.

3. Composition and mood

Composition: A massive, angular station entrance fills the bottom and middle of the image. Its still geometry contrasts with a disorderly spiral of birds overhead. The portal divides the built world from an open sky. The lines of the building catch the eye and lead it upwards towards the birds.

Mood: The picture is contemplative and has a modern romantic quality. There is energy and expectation despite the subdued colour. The rising flock can feel both unsettling and liberating, like a departure that holds the sadness of goodbye and the excitement of what lies ahead.

Editorial translation of the submitted 2025 text

1. Work description (context)

The Overflowing Night captures a timeless, deeply atmospheric moment in the nightlife of a historic European city. At the centre of the photograph is the encounter between monumental architecture and a traditional means of transport: the Fiaker, or horse-drawn carriage. The work does more than document a scene. It gives visual form to an idea: “The Overflowing Night shows that darkness cannot hold the city back. Light, movement and history flow on without end, urged forward by timeless carriages.” History lives on here, and the aesthetics of the past breathe in the light of the present.

2. Title, technique and philosophy

The title refers to the unceasing flow of light and energy that breaks through the darkness.

Technique: The photograph was made in low light and plays on warm tones and strong contrasts. The slight motion blur of the horse pulling the carriage shows that the photographer used a slow shutter speed. It conveys movement continuing in the otherwise motionless stillness of the night. The deliberately dark, golden-brown palette gives the picture a painterly, romantic atmosphere, as if it were illuminated by an old, warm lamp.

Philosophy: The carriage stands for timelessness and historical continuity. What makes the city itself, its history and its pulsing life, is stronger than the dark. The deliberately highlighted lamps and windows embody knowledge, life and hope beneath the monumental colonnade and dark sky.

3. Composition and mood

Composition: The dominant arc of the colonnade on the left establishes the stability of classical forms. The carriage moves diagonally in the lower-right quarter, bringing energy to the image. The grand mass of architecture on the left is balanced by the fleeting movement of the horse on the right. Rough cobblestones in the foreground contrast with the smooth building, giving the scene depth and a tactile quality.

Mood: The image is deeply romantic, nostalgic and rich. Warm, dark colours and subtle contrasts give it a theatrical, dramatic quality. The nighttime scene is intimate, as though the viewer had witnessed a secret rendezvous and the carriage carried the secrets of times past. The photograph invites a slow, meditative journey into a labyrinth of history and light.

1. Work description (context)

Outside Vienna’s Tichy ice-cream parlour, the photograph reflects on childhood joy and the way a remembered moment can feel timeless. It combines the energy of the city at night with a sense of carefree happiness. The lit façade and neon signs frame movement, lightness and pleasure in being alive. This is an ein Stück Sommerglück, a little piece of summer happiness. The imagined scent of fresh ice cream hangs over the scene like a sweet memory on the border between past and present.

2. Title, technique and philosophy

Tichy – ein Stück Sommerglück names the place and the moment of happiness associated with it.

Technique: Black and white brings out the depth and elegance of the light and shadow. A noisy, colourful setting takes on the feel of a classic film noir. Light picks out the building’s surfaces and the neon signage, which seems to float in the dark. The blur of laughing children running through the space makes their freedom visible and shows how quickly it can pass. As the source puts it, “Und für einen Augenblick bleibt die Zeit stehen”: for an instant, time stands still.

Philosophy: The space in front of Tichy becomes a sweet memory of carefree days. The photograph sets the lightness of movement against the illusion that black and white can hold a moment outside time.

3. Composition and mood

Composition: The corner of the building makes a diagonal towards the centre. The Tichy neon and the vertical EISSALON sign are focal points. The detail of the architecture contrasts with children in motion in the foreground. Their blurred forms and the positions of other passers-by give the building depth and a human scale.

Mood: The picture is playful and nostalgic as well as reflective. Even without colour it evokes summer happiness. The night lights feel warm and inviting, and the children’s movement gives the image something of the freedom of a dream. For a moment, it feels as though time really has stopped.

1. Work description (context)

The photograph shows Vienna’s Riesenrad, the giant Ferris wheel in the Prater, from an unusual point of view and in black and white. More than a view of a fairground attraction, it uses the wheel to think about life, memory and the passing of time. In the photographer’s words: “The Ferris wheel reminds me that everything passes and returns. Like thoughts, like feelings, it keeps turning even if I stop for a moment.”

2. Title, technique and philosophy

The title connects the physical movement of the wheel with the recurring movement of thoughts and feelings.

Technique: Black and white puts the emphasis on the clarity of form, light and geometry. Strong contrast between the sky and the structure, perhaps intensified in editing, makes it dramatic. A close view looking up from below makes the wheel feel imposing, almost threatening. Cropping part of it out loosens its connection to the fairground and gives it a broader meaning.

Philosophy: The wheel is a sign of cyclical time. It keeps moving and returning like thoughts and feelings. A person may pause in the rush of life, but time, the world and our inward processes keep turning without us.

3. Composition and mood

Composition: Sweeping curves fill much of the picture and draw the eye in. Diagonals in the metal framework make it feel charged, while the strong geometry of the lattice and cabins contrasts with a plain, empty sky. The cabins carry visual weight and add tension.

Mood: The picture is sombre, absorbing and reflective. Monochrome creates a distance that suits its meditation on time. The scale of the wheel and the deep shadows suggest something strong and enduring, against which we can consider the brevity of a human life.

Artwork

Exhibited photograph 09

Prices in the submitted 2025 text are archival, not current offers.

Editorial translation of the submitted 2025 text

1. Work description (context)

This is an intimate portrait of the Schönbrunn park, where the formality of a historic place meets the texture of personal experience. “Schönbrunn and my life in Vienna are inseparable,” says the source. The gate is a physical barrier and at the same time a window into history, culture, beauty and the quiet pleasures of family and friendship. Walks before, during and after Covid, dates, pushing a pram and visiting Christmas markets come together in a single autumn moment.

2. Title, technique and philosophy

The title puts the particular time of an autumn Sunday against the endurance of the place.

Technique: High-contrast black and white sets autumn colour aside in favour of form, texture and the play of light and shade. The wrought-iron gate and stone stair rail form a close foreground frame. The depth and the sense of peering through suggest a glimpse into a private place or a book of memories. Shooting into the light turns metal and stone into silhouettes and lends the distant architecture, possibly the Gloriette, an otherworldly glow.

Philosophy: The gate holds both exclusion, recalling the experience of finding it closed at night, and an intimacy without bounds. Personal experience happens within the formal structure of history. The photographer can think of Schönbrunn as a garden of their own, a private inward space, while people walking beyond the gate remind us that it is a shared inheritance.

3. Composition and mood

Composition: The diagonal stone rail and the wrought-iron ornament define the sides of the frame. The frame within the frame guides the eye into a brighter central area, where small walking figures occupy an open space. Historic architecture anchors the distance.

Mood: The atmosphere is nostalgic, intimate and quietly ceremonial. In black and white, the walkers and railings seem as though they could have stepped out of the age of Sisi and Franz Joseph. The spirals of the gate introduce a lightness beside the stone. The picture conveys a lasting attachment to, and respect for, Schönbrunn.

Editorial translation of the submitted 2025 text

Our common humanity: Megtévesztően Hasonló (Deceptively Similar)

For Naylivart, the photograph shows more than what is visible at first glance. The women’s simple, concealing clothes resemble one another in form. That resemblance invites the viewer to look beyond outward things such as a garment, a symbol or a tradition, and to recognise what connects people.

In a world that often stresses differences, the photograph asks us to notice how alike our most basic needs are.

Love and belonging: We all want to love and be loved. Being accepted unconditionally by a family, a community or the world around us is part of what makes us happy.

Recognition: People want their dignity and contributions to be recognised. Each of us wants to be seen as a person whose voice and actions matter, whatever our religion or background.

Freedom and choice: What someone chooses to wear or not wear should remain a personal decision, free from coercion or outside judgement. The freedom to make our own choices, including how we dress, matters to everyone.

Peace: The desire to live in harmony is a common ground. “Live and let live” is not simply a formula for tolerance but a way to practise love and respect for others.

The women are not shown as individually identifiable. In looking at them, the work asks us to recognise what we share: hopes and fears and values that can outweigh our differences.

It is an invitation to stand together and to pay less attention to what divides us than to what connects us: our common humanity.

Nagy Márton

Artwork

Exhibited photograph 05

Prices in the submitted 2025 text are archival, not current offers.

Editorial translation of the submitted 2025 text

Price listed in 2025: €250. Not a current offer.

1. Work description (context)

A Shelby Cobra stands against the cobblestones and historic architecture of Vienna, apparently in the city centre. The bold shape of the American car sets speed in conversation with old European streets. Chrome and bright blue paint meet stone and weathered plaster. To the photographer the car stands for an uncompromising way of driving and a sudden flash of wild energy in an ordinary street. It suggests freedom, adrenaline and the remembered excitement of another era.

2. Title, technique and philosophy

The title sets the name Cobra against the classical setting of Vienna.

Technique: Saturated, slightly stylised colour brings out the deep blue paint and white racing stripes. The close, low viewpoint makes the aggressive lines feel imposing. The shallow depth of field keeps the car sharp against a soft but recognisable background. Sharp cobblestones add texture in the foreground.

Philosophy: The work is about freedom and things that outlast their moment. The Cobra is an icon of analogue driving, a reminder of mechanical beauty and human performance in a digital world. Against the old street it suggests that style and passion can carry the excitement of the past into the present. A shape built for speed among people walking slowly makes the car seem both out of place and perfectly at home.

3. Composition and mood

Composition: The Cobra lies diagonally across the street, giving a still image movement. Its nose points towards the centre, suggesting a way forward. The low, wide form offsets the verticals of the old buildings. Small figures along the street give the car a sense of scale.

Mood: The picture is adventurous, elegant and slightly tense. Bright blue and strong contrast convey adrenaline and excitement. Time seems to stop while a symbol of speed rests amid history. Raw power and classical splendour occupy the same frame.

About the artist

2. Title, technique and philosophy

The title brings together the act of holding time still and the colours that make it visible.

Technique: The photograph makes use of warm natural autumn light. Its vertical format emphasises the height of the tree and the open sky. The shutter speed was probably fast enough to hold some leaves sharp while allowing others to show movement. Saturated orange, gold and deep blue convey the colours of time. Backlighting renders part of the figure in silhouette and draws attention to the interplay of figure and light.

Philosophy: Autumn marks a recurring passage between beauty and decay, endings and beginnings. Falling leaves embody the passing of time. The woman’s lifted, open arms suggest a lasting commitment to love and nature. The photograph proposes that some values endure amid continual change.

3. Composition and mood

Composition: Strong diagonals and contrasting bands divide the picture. A warm yellow carpet of leaves lies under a clear blue sky, and a dark tree trunk frames the right side. At the centre, the woman becomes the focus of the movement as airborne leaves fill the space around her.

Mood: The image feels free, energetic and lyrical. Intense colour and sunlight suggest happiness and optimism. The lifted hands express joy and surrender, as though making peace with the passage of time. The whole picture reads as a warm, celebratory moment of reflection in the comfort of nature.

Mood: The picture is quiet, reflective and majestic. Gold and orange convey warmth and nostalgia; the dark frame and the almost empty setting add melancholy. Time seems to stop briefly, inviting the viewer to enter the light and follow an inward path of their own.

1. Work description (context)

The picture reflects on the calm nature offers and on being quietly in someone else’s company. It was made at sunset on the shore of Lake Neusiedl (Neusiedler See), in front of the Podersdorf lighthouse. Gold, orange and deep blue are reflected in the water. The photographer’s own words describe the feeling: “Watching the sunsets and the surface of the lake makes me calm.” The lighthouse, a human landmark, stands against the changing splendour of nature. Three figures on a bench suggest the value of stillness shared.

2. Title, technique and philosophy

The title connects a sunset with a place that carries an emotional meaning.

Technique: The image uses the passage from golden hour to blue hour. A yellow, orange and red band stretches along a sharp horizon, between deep blue above and the cool, neutral concrete below. A long exposure or still water may account for the mirror-smooth lake. The dark pier and lighthouse are strong silhouettes against the bright sky.

Philosophy: The lighthouse suggests safety and return against the end of a day. Recurring events in nature, such as sunset, can offer a refuge from the noise of modern life. Stillness is at the centre of the picture, in the water and in the figures sitting close together without moving.

3. Composition and mood

Composition: Three broad horizontal bands divide the frame: concrete, then pier and water, then sky. The first anchors the scene, the second gives it depth and the third sets the mood. The lighthouse and the seated figures sit in the right-hand third, where the diagonal lines of the pier lead the eye.

Mood: The photograph is peaceful, melancholy and romantic. The contrast between orange and the darkening sky carries both the sadness of a day ending and the promise of another. Contemplating the lake, the figures suggest the solitude it is possible to feel in company.

1. Work description (context)

This surreal, high-contrast black-and-white composition alters scale and the human form to create emotional tension. A pointed, almost geometric shape, described in the source as a large, textured female breast, dominates an enclosed, minimal space. Two tiny, indistinct human figures appear inside it. Putting an intimate part of the body on a monumental scale brings questions of human relationships, fragility and maternal or emotional shelter into view.

2. Title, technique and philosophy

The title points to the deliberate disruption of ordinary proportions.

Technique: The picture suggests a montage or collage that combines life-sized and miniature elements to unsettle what looks real. Extreme contrast takes the subject away from ordinary appearances and towards an abstract form. Hard sidelight casts deep shadows and gives the form a sculptural, rough texture. A white outer frame, perhaps paper or part of a structure, separates the drama inside from a neutral space outside.

Philosophy: The monumental form can be read as an archetype of safety, motherhood, nourishment and feminine power. The two small figures suggest the vulnerability of life in the face of forces larger than itself. The cold geometry and enclosure imply that shelter has boundaries, too, and that relationships can be difficult to find one’s way through.

3. Composition and mood

Composition: The central form cuts diagonally through the inner image, making a bright, textured relief and a deeply shadowed corner. Two miniature figures along the diagonal establish the difference in scale. The broad white border, like a window or a wall opening, makes it feel as if one photograph has been placed inside another.

Mood: The image is oppressive, tense and estranging, but also mysterious. Its contrast and distorted scale can cause unease and fascination at once. The vulnerability of the figures suggests the weight of fate and things hidden from conscious view. The picture has the tension of a quiet dream.
